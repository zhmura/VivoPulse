**VivoPulse: project exploration, DSP, biomechanics and measurement validity**

Historical audit (before implementation). Research date: 21 September 2026. Repository inspected: main, commit 02999ad. This is a source review and targeted web literature review, not a systematic review, device experiment, or clinical validation. No application code was changed and no Android build or test suite was run. One isolated analytical check of the spectral-delay expressions was performed. Subsequent fixes and executed tests are recorded in [the implementation record](MEASUREMENT_VALIDITY_IMPLEMENTATION.md); statements below refer to the original commit. Source links are pinned to that revision.

**Assessment.** VivoPulse contains a substantial Android research implementation for simultaneous facial rPPG and fingertip contact PPG. Its most defensible immediate objective is a reproducible measurement of optical inter-site pulse delay with documented uncertainty and failure conditions. The current implementation has correctness and provenance defects that should be resolved before collecting validation data. Blood-pressure, arterial-stiffness and clinical HRV interpretations each require additional, distinct evidence.

**The problem and current implementation.** The functional specification proposes 30–60 second dual-camera sessions, quality coaching, on-device processing, pulse timing, waveform features and exports; it explicitly excludes BP, cfPWV and AIx claims. The interview pitch makes stronger BP-related comparisons. The specification is a better statement of the current research scope. See [functional specification](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/docs/FUNCTIONAL_SPEC.md#L3) and [interview pitch](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/docs/PTT_Interview_Pitch.md#L7).

There is a real motivation for more accessible cardiovascular measurement: WHO reports 1.4 billion people with hypertension in 2024, with just over one fifth controlled. That burden does not establish that a phone-derived pulse delay is a valid BP measurement. [WHO, 2025](https://www.who.int/news/item/23-09-2025-uncontrolled-high-blood-pressure-puts-over-a-billion-people-at-risk).

**Define the medical quantity before choosing an algorithm.** Different outputs require different references and acceptance criteria.

| Intended output | What the phone directly observes | Appropriate validation endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse rate / mean HR estimate | Periodic optical blood-volume changes | Simultaneous ECG/reference rate over exactly matching windows; beat detection and failure rate. |
| Pulse rate variability, PRV | Intervals between optical pulse landmarks | Simultaneous ECG intervals and PRV; separate RMSSD/SDNN agreement by duration, condition and artifact burden. |
| Face–finger differential transit time, dPTT | Difference between two optical arrival/landmark times | Synchronized reference PPG at corresponding sites; signed delay error, repeatability and accepted coverage. |
| Arterial stiffness / PWV | Not directly identified by these two camera signals | Defined arterial path length and transit measurement, or a separately validated inference against an appropriate PWV reference. |
| Systolic/diastolic BP | Not directly measured by the current pipeline | A prespecified, calibrated or calibration-free model evaluated against an appropriate BP reference in the intended population and conditions. |

ECG-to-pulse timing is PAT and includes pre-ejection timing. Simultaneous face–finger timing is more precisely a difference between two heart-to-site transit times, dPTT. The face and finger lie on different arterial branches; this is not blood travelling from face to finger. Block et al. explicitly distinguish these quantities. Their 32-person intervention study found that different timing definitions tracked BP differently; removing pre-ejection timing did not guarantee superior BP tracking. [Block et al., Scientific Reports, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7532447/).

A useful engineering observation model is:

    observed optical delay ≈
        (heart-to-finger transit − heart-to-face transit)
        + differential camera timing
        + optical/landmark/processing bias
        + measurement noise

This decomposition is a working model, not a calibrated physiological equation. Each added term needs measurement or a defensible bound.

For arterial mechanics, the Bramwell–Hill relationship can be written c² = (A/ρ)·dP/dA. An idealized thin-wall model gives c² ≈ E_inc·h/(ρ·D), with transit approximately the integral of ds/c along an arterial path. Pressure, wall stiffness, geometry and vascular tone are coupled. Neither relation supplies a unique absolute BP from an arbitrary optical lag. Ma et al. examined pressure–PWV relationships using constitutive modeling and an in-vitro simulator, illustrating the importance of model assumptions. [Ma et al., PNAS, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6217416/).

Consequently, dividing an estimated body distance by face–finger delay would not establish carotid–femoral PWV. The 2024 PWV recommendations require explicit pathway/reference definitions and distinguish measured from inferred parameters. Their reference-system acquisition guidance is not satisfied by upsampling a low-frame-rate video. [Multi-society PWV validation recommendations, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10734786/).

**Biomechanical controls should come before more complicated estimation.**

Finger pressure is particularly consequential. In 17 healthy subjects, changing PPG contact pressure shifted PAT by up to 22 ± 2 ms at the foot and 40 ± 7 ms at the peak. These were observed timing changes; they should not be converted into a universal BP error using a fixed slope. [Chandrasekhar et al., IEEE TBME, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8856571/).

Development recommendations inferred from the evidence:

- Measure contact force/pressure with a fixture or sensor during validation. A saturation indicator and “press gently” instruction do not establish stable pressure.
- Standardize posture, hand position relative to the heart, finger placement and acclimatization. Record these variables and test their effects independently.
- Retain separate anatomical facial ROIs. Changing the mixture of forehead and cheek signals may change phase even when HR stays constant.
- Preserve the selected color channel, raw channel traces and exposure information. A dual-depth PPG experiment found wavelength-dependent waveform/phase differences affected by compression; face green versus finger chrominance therefore needs direct comparison. [Moço et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30441494/).
- Describe the rear-camera setup as contact camera PPG. Calling it a conventional transmissive clip sensor is not justified without characterizing its source–tissue–detector geometry.
- Treat persistent changes as unexplained measurement changes until repeatability and biological specificity are established. “Relative to your baseline” does not by itself remove these confounders.

**The DSP should preserve the information required by the endpoint.** Accurate mean pulse rate can coexist with inaccurate pulse feet, waveform shape and inter-site delay. The following are candidate design choices to validate, not proven upgrades for VivoPulse.

| Stage / method | Current approach and useful property | Recommendation for timing validity |
|---|---|---|
| RGB extraction: green, CHROM, POS | Green proxy and finger chrominance feed the main pipeline; POS exists separately. POS/CHROM suppress some illumination/motion effects. | Benchmark raw color traces, faithful POS and CHROM using delay error as well as HR/SNR. Fix the channel/ROI rule before evaluation. Time-varying channel combinations can change landmarks. |
| Timestamps and resampling | Absolute timestamp overlap plus linear interpolation; adaptive processing grid. | Verify shared clock and exposure timing. Preserve native timestamps and gap masks. Permit interpolation only within a validated gap bound. A 100 Hz grid does not turn 30 fps acquisition into a 100 Hz sensor. |
| Detrending and bandpass | Causal IIR detrending followed by forward/backward nominal 0.7–4 Hz filtering; the complete chain is therefore not zero phase. | Use one explicit sampling-rate value throughout. Verify the complete frequency/phase response and edge behavior. A rate-estimation branch can be narrower than a morphology branch; select timing bandwidth empirically. |
| Wavelets and change-point repair | Conditional Haar denoising and linear replacement around detected steps. | Test each stage against known timing. Preserve repair masks and exclude reconstructed beats from physiological estimates. Do not score an interpolated segment as observed physiology. |
| Motion handling / notch filtering | Motion rejection, IMU features and optional step-frequency notch filtering. | Prefer rejection of invalid intervals in the first validated resting protocol. Motion can overlap the pulse band; a notch may remove true pulse information. Evaluate active cancellation only with a suitable independent nuisance reference. |
| NCC and sub-sample peak interpolation | Robust window-level similarity measure. | Keep as an interpretable baseline. Prespecify sign, lag range, polarity and ambiguity rejection. Broad/periodic peaks or changing morphology undermine delay identifiability despite high correlation. |
| Foot-to-foot timing | Intersecting-tangent foot estimation using a derivative. | Compare tangent, derivative and fractional-upstroke fiducials against reference feet. Require observable landmarks, monotonic same-beat pairing and artifact rejection. Validate at native frame rates; interpolation alone cannot justify enabling foot detection. |
| GCC-PHAT | Spectral normalization can sharpen a correlation peak. | Regularize weak spectral bins and compare against NCC; whitening can emphasize unreliable bins. Demonstrate improvement in signed delay agreement, not just peak sharpness. |
| Cross-spectral phase delay | Coherence-weighted phase fitting around pulse harmonics. | Fix the sign defect below. Check phase residuals, independent spectral information, wrapping and waveform-shape differences. Three neighboring bins do not necessarily supply three independent observations. |
| Fusion and confidence | Scalar Kalman fusion with heuristic variances, prior and quality scores. | Establish a valid single-method baseline first. Shared data makes estimator errors correlated, and different landmarks need not measure an identical delay. Validate covariance and interval coverage before presenting a 95% interval. |
| PRV / waveform features | RMSSD, SDNN, rise time, reflection-like ratio and dicrotic hint. | Give each metric its own duration, quality and reference validation. A clean sinusoid cannot establish preserved dicrotic morphology or arterial stiffness. |

POS's original evaluation emphasizes pulse spectral SNR; CHROM's evidence emphasizes pulse-rate robustness. These are appropriate baselines, but neither publication establishes millisecond delay accuracy for VivoPulse. [Wang et al., 2017](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/algorithmic-principles-of-remote-ppg/), [de Haan & Jeanne, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23744659/).

A small six-volunteer comparison found useful precision from intersecting tangents, while explicitly recognizing morphology-related foot shifts between measurement sites. It supports comparing fiducials rather than assuming one is universally correct. [Phillips & Kyriacou, EMBC 2014](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/13364/1/IEEE%20EMBC%202014%20Chicago%20Justin.pdf).

At 30 fps, adjacent frames are approximately 33.3 ms apart. Sub-frame estimates are possible under suitable signal/noise assumptions, so 33.3 ms is not an absolute lower bound on every averaged estimator. Nevertheless, finer numerical output is not evidence of finer measurement accuracy.

Android timestamps refer to first-row exposure start; REALTIME and UNKNOWN timestamp sources have different comparability guarantees. Rolling shutter and ROI position need characterization. A possible approximation is exposure-start time + sensor-row fraction × rolling-shutter skew + half the exposure duration, with correct crop/orientation mapping. This is an engineering approximation that must be optically verified. [Android Camera2 timing definitions](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/camera2/CaptureResult), [timestamp-source characteristics](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/camera2/CameraCharacteristics#SENSOR_INFO_TIMESTAMP_SOURCE).

A six-smartphone optical-phantom experiment observed apparent spatial timing shifts up to 20 ms without a true source delay. That is an experimental artifact scale, not a specification for every phone. [Mironenko et al., CVPRW 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12695).

For the spectral sign finding, an isolated calculation with ideal harmonic phases corresponding to an 80 ms finger delay returned −80 ms using the two expressions above. This verifies their inconsistency algebraically; it is not a Kotlin integration test or a claim that the final UI always displays −80 ms.

Two further review concerns affect interpretation. Clock “drift” is inferred from the ratio of total recording spans, which can change merely because streams start/stop at different times ([span ratio](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/feature-processing/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/feature/processing/timestamp/TimestampSync.kt#L313)). Kalman fusion resets to a 100 ms prior for each estimate ([fusion reset](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/feature-processing/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/feature/processing/ptt/PTTConsensus.kt#L115)); its displayed uncertainty is conditional on heuristic model assumptions and has no demonstrated clinical coverage in the reviewed material.

Estimator failure semantics also need attention. GCC excludes the central ±30 ms region and can mark a result valid solely because the input is long enough ([GCC search](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/feature-processing/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/feature/processing/ptt/CrossCorr.kt#L238)). Foot matching chooses a nearest finger foot independently for each face foot, without enforcing unique matches ([foot pairing](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/feature-processing/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/feature/processing/ptt/IntersectingTangentFoot.kt#L145)). A fusion call with no accepted measurements can still return its prior ([fusion output](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/feature-processing/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/feature/processing/ptt/PttKalmanFuser.kt#L60)). A defensible measurement must permit zero delay, ambiguity and no result where appropriate; physiological priors cannot substitute for observed evidence.

For numerical verification, use an independent implementation as an oracle, with pinned parameters and versions. SciPy documents SOS Butterworth design and configurable Savitzky–Golay derivatives; equivalence tests should cover responses, boundaries and time units rather than mirror the Kotlin implementation. [Butterworth reference](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.butter.html), [Savitzky–Golay reference](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.savgol_filter.html).

**The proposed measurement architecture separates acquisition validity from physiological estimation.**

    Real simultaneous streams + provenance
        → verified timebase, exposure metadata and native cadence
        → stable ROIs, color traces, pressure/motion observations
        → artifact masks and accepted contiguous intervals
        → independently validated branches:
            pulse rate | pulse intervals | waveform/foot timing
        → signed dPTT and method disagreement
        → empirical uncertainty and explicit unavailable states
        → auditable export
        → separately developed clinical inference, if justified

A practical initial comparator is fixed preprocessing plus one foot method and NCC reported separately, with disagreement causing review/rejection. Additional methods should earn inclusion by reducing held-out error without hiding rejection rates. Do not optimize quality thresholds only until plausible-looking numbers appear.

**Optical variability deserves separate treatment.** In the current biomarker code, RMSSD/SDNN eligibility requires at least ten intervals plus generic quality thresholds ([BiomarkerComputer](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/feature-processing/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/feature/processing/biomarker/Biomarkers.kt#L28)). That is not a duration-specific validation protocol, and the detected intervals are optical pulse intervals rather than ECG R–R intervals. Use PRV terminology, preserve artifact flags and establish whether each metric agrees with ECG in the intended use.

A 2025 clinical-population study found material differences between PPG PRV and ECG HRV. Short-recording evidence also depends on measurement modality and careful artifact handling: Muñoz et al. studied 3,387 adults using Portapres finger-pressure pulse intervals, with duration-dependent agreement and better short-recording behavior for RMSSD than SDNN. Neither study validates ten intervals from this camera pipeline. [Kantrowitz et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40809286/), [Muñoz et al., 2015, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4586373/).

**Alternative approaches are worth testing when they add observability.**

| Approach | Potential benefit | Remaining requirement |
|---|---|---|

| Finger oscillometry with measured pressure | Adds an external pressure measurement and a mechanical pressure–volume relationship. A 2018 study evaluated a smartphone-associated PPG/force system in 32 people for accuracy. | Requires suitable force/pressure sensing, pressure-transfer characterization and its own BP validation; not a software-only upgrade. [Chandrasekhar et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29515001/). |
| SCG/BCG plus peripheral PPG | Mechanical cardiac timing may provide another proximal reference. | Requires reliable mechanical fiducials and motion control. Scale-based BCG results do not validate a handheld phone IMU. [Martin et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5157040/). |
| Upstroke features with an interpretable calibrated model | Can test whether morphology contributes beyond a timing scalar. | Beat morphology must first be valid; evaluate incremental performance over simpler baselines. | Learned rPPG or BP models | May improve robustness within represented acquisition domains. | Training/evaluation must separate participants, sessions and devices. Compare with calibration-only, HR and demographic baselines; verify actual dynamic BP tracking. |
| Higher native FPS / dedicated synchronized optics | Improves sampling and may simplify reference experiments. | Confirm concurrent-camera, exposure and thermal behavior; higher FPS alone does not resolve physiology or clock/ROI biases. |

MMPD provides mobile-video research data spanning skin tones, lighting and motion. It is useful for evaluating facial extraction, not a substitute for synchronized VivoPulse face–finger validation. Its current repository restricts use to academic purposes and forbids commercial use; check access terms before adoption. [MMPD paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03840), [dataset access terms](https://github.com/THU-CS-PI/MMPD_rPPG_dataset).

**A validation program should establish five things in sequence.** The following is a proposed research plan, not a claim that a particular numeric threshold is medically accepted.

1. **Numerical correctness.** Repair provenance, gate propagation, sign, sample-rate and SQI defects. Build deterministic tests for known positive/negative/zero delay, unequal rates, missing channels, clock offsets, long gaps, clipping, flatlines, uncorrelated noise and morphology changes. Verify each estimator separately. Add polynomial/filter-response checks and ensure rejected measurements stay rejected at UI/export boundaries.

2. **Camera metrology.** Use a hardware-timed optical stimulus visible to both cameras, including known zero and programmable delays. Vary ROI row, orientation, exposure, native FPS, camera model, dropped frames, load and temperature. Independently characterize stimulus/reference timing. Estimate offset, drift and uncertainty; test whether any calibration remains stable. Log timestamp-source metadata instead of inferring synchronization from similar stream lengths.

3. **Physiological timing agreement.** Record research PPG at matching/nearby facial and finger sites on a common high-rate acquisition clock; add ECG for beat identity and a pressure sensor for contact characterization. Quantify limitations of imperfect sensor co-location and modality. Match analysis windows exactly. Prespecify the delay landmark and sign; report per-beat and session errors independently.

4. **Repeatability and confounders.** Repeat recordings on different days/devices and across skin pigmentation, illumination, age and relevant physiology. Separately vary pressure, posture/hand height, ROI and mild motion under an appropriate study protocol. Report systematic bias, limits of agreement, subject-level repeatability, rejected sessions, accepted duration and subgroup uncertainty. Avoid treating many beats from one participant as independent participants.

5. **Clinical interpretation.** Only after signal/timing validity is established, test the intended relationship to BP, stiffness or another endpoint using an appropriate reference and clinical collaborators. Keep calibration observations separate from evaluation, test change tracking over time, and lock the model before external evaluation. Determine sample size and acceptable error from the intended clinical use and statistical precision, not the sizes of favorable pilot studies.

Uncertainty should include residual camera calibration error, optical/fiducial uncertainty, algorithm sensitivity and repeatability. Bootstrap contiguous beats/windows or participants as appropriate; do not create artificial certainty by treating interpolated points or correlated estimators as independent measurements. Select an engineering delay-error target based on the smallest change the proposed use needs to distinguish, then justify it clinically. There is no universal “medically correct PTT” threshold supplied by the current project.

For future BP claims, the ESH 2023 recommendations describe static, device-position, treatment, awake/asleep, exercise and recalibration tests; which apply depends on device operation, calibration and intended positions. The AHA's December 2025 statement emphasizes calibration and real-world validation. FDA's January 2026 cuffless guidance remains labeled draft/nonbinding at the reviewed page. These sources inform study design; none certifies VivoPulse. [ESH recommendations](https://www.arc-oxtv.nihr.ac.uk/publications/1393612/), [AHA statement summary](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/cuffless-devices-for-the-measurement-of-blood-pressure), [FDA draft guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/cuffless-non-invasive-blood-pressure-measuring-devices-clinical-performance-testing-and-evaluation).

**Exports must support reproducible evaluation.** The active export encrypts an archive using an Android-keystore-backed key, but the inspected UI only reports a path and no complete recipient decrypt/share workflow was found. More importantly for research, exported app version, session timing and drift contain fixed/reconstructed values, and the cross-correlation plot is a text placeholder. See [export metadata](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/app/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/app/viewmodel/ProcessingViewModel.kt#L275), [export invocation](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/app/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/app/viewmodel/ProcessingViewModel.kt#L384), [encryption](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/core-io/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/io/ClinicianGradeExporter.kt#L163) and [plot placeholder](https://github.com/zhmura/VivoPulse/blob/02999ad/core-io/src/main/java/com/vivopulse/io/ClinicianGradeExporter.kt#L436).

Export original timestamps and RGB traces, actual device/camera modes, exposure/ISO, native FPS and drops, ROI coordinates, pressure/motion observations, all masks, algorithm version/parameters, per-method estimates, rejection reasons, reference synchronization and explicit real/synthetic provenance. Missing metadata should be null/unknown rather than zero. Confirm that a recipient can recover and inspect the archive.

**Recommended order of work.** First make measurement provenance and invalid-result handling reliable. Next correct the numerical defects and establish independent algorithm checks. Then build a camera timing fixture and a synchronized human reference dataset. Use those data to select preprocessing, fiducials, quality thresholds and uncertainty. Evaluate clinical inference only after the observable itself is accurate and repeatable. The immediate milestone should be a reproducible table of delay error and measurement availability across defined conditions, with exports sufficient for independent reanalysis.
